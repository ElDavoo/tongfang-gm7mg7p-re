# `0x09E9`-`0x09EB` — the 9 EC reference sites, and the four-hop GPU boost chain they close

`ec/annotations/ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md` walked the 15 main-EC sites of
`0x07C4`-`0x07D5` and established that the routine entered at `0x83FF`
copies `0x09EA`->`CPUA` (`0x07D4`) and `0x09EB`->`DBAP` (`0x07D5`). It named
what produced those two bytes as the open question, and this file is that
question: the 9 direct `MOV DPTR` sites of `0x09E9`, `0x09EA` and `0x09EB` in
the EC image, and what the three of them add up to.

**The short answer.** Three sites each, all in the EC, none in the PD image.
`0x09EA` and `0x09EB` have exactly **one direct writer each**, both the same
one: the routine entered at `0x96AD` copies `0x0745` and `0x0746` into them
when `CTGP_DB_CTRL` (`0x0743`) bit 0 is set. Their other two sites each are
reads in the routine entered at `0x83FF`. So the two middle hops of the
GPU dynamic-boost path each live in one routine, and the whole chain — the
Windows service's power-mode write, `0x96AD`, `0x83FF`, and the ASL that
publishes to the NVIDIA platform controller — is four hops and is drawn once,
here, in §4. `0x09E9` has **two** direct writers and a different question; it
is walked here for the reconciliation but is deliberately **not** given a
`registers.yaml` row in this change (§6).

**None of that is a live test.** These are statements about an 8-instruction
linear window around each site, plus the routine each one sits in, plus the
control flow between them walked by hand. A window that stops at a branch is
*this method stopping*, never "this site does not access the byte" — the
`ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md` §5 discipline. Indirect `movx @Ri` and a `DPH` built
at run time are invisible to the scan; §3 handles the one `DPH`-building site
aimed at this page and says what it does and does not license. `0x09EA` and
`0x09EB` are `present-untested`: these bytes have never been watched at all,
let alone exercised, and nothing in §5 changes that.

three `static_refs` / `static_refs_main_ec` / `static_refs_pd_image` values in
`ec/annotations/registers.yaml` are the same numbers, and the file that

All 9 are `bank0` and **none** is in the `ITE8850-PD` image, so every

unlike `0x07D4`/`0x07D5`, whose counts are 68- and 24-fold PD
(`ec-0x07d0-sites.md` §1, and the calibration note in `ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md`
§2). Here the EC-side count *is* the whole count.

**Six reads, three writes, over two routines and one stub.** `0x83FF` and
`0x96AD` hold eight of the nine; the ninth is `0x9C0D`, in the routine entered
at `0x9C00`, already named `clear_09e9_unless_0743_bit2` in
[`ghidra-functions.csv`](ghidra-functions.csv) — it stores `0x00` to `0x09E9`
when bit 2 of `0x0743` is clear. All nine sites carry `frame_onto: 24` in the
CSV, so the 24 preceding anchors converge onto every one of them and the
column is not load-bearing for any claim here.

**Read off the `.asm`, not off the decompiled C.** A reader counting `=`
signs in `ec/decompiled/bank0/` would count `0x09EA` and `0x09EB` as having
more sites than they do, because Ghidra's decompiler emitted one routine's
body into more than one exported file:

- `96AD.c:52-53` and `95DD.c:111-112` both contain
  `DAT_EXTMEM_09ea = CTGP_DB_TPP_TARGET;`. `95DD.asm` ends at `0x96AC`, one
  byte before `0x96AD`, so the write at `0x96F8` is not in it: the decompiler
  ran the body straight through the repository's `0x96AD` function boundary.
  **One** write site, two `.c` files.
- `83FF.c:44-45`, `8038.c` and `8044.c` spell the same two reads. `8038` is
  the head of an eight-entry dispatch table, not a function, and `8044` is a
  single `AJMP 0x845D` into the middle of `0x83FF` — both already described
  that way in their own `ghidra-functions.csv` rows.

`xdata-registers.csv` inherits the same five-function overlap (its `0x09EA`
row names `0x8038`, `0x8044`, `0x83FF`, `0x95DD` and `0x96AD`), which is why
its `refs: 7` is not 3. This file's per-address table and the CSV are the
census for these three addresses.

**Not writers, and not even addresses.** `ec/decompiled/common/09E9.*` is a
*code* address in the common area, not this XDATA byte; the `0x09E9x`-shaped
rows in `bank-paged-call-targets.csv`, `bank-relative-branch-targets.csv` and
`xdata-086x-dispatch-sites.csv` are the same confusion. None is a reference
to XDATA `0x09E9`.

## 3. A computed `DPH` onto this page: one hit, and what it bounds

`ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md` §6 sets out the blind spot that matters here: a `MOV
DPTR,#addr` scan cannot see a DPTR built at run time, and the EC does build
one — `addc a,#0xNN ; mov DPH,a` (`34 NN F5 83`) reaches the `0x0F00` page
eight times. Aimed at this page the pattern is `34 09 F5 83`:

```console
$ python3 - <<'EOF'
from pathlib import Path
ec = Path('ec/firmware/GMxMGxx_11.800').read_bytes()[:0x20000]
for page in (0x07, 0x09):
    pat = bytes([0x34, page, 0xf5, 0x83])
    hits = [i for i in range(len(ec) - 3) if ec[i:i+4] == pat]
    print(f"addc a,#0x{page:02x} ; mov DPH,a -> {len(hits)}: "
          + ", ".join(f"0x{i:04X}" for i in hits))
EOF
addc a,#0x07 ; mov DPH,a -> 0:
addc a,#0x09 ; mov DPH,a -> 1: 0x83EA
```

**One hit, not zero, and it is in a routine this file does not otherwise walk.**
`0x83EA` sits in the routine entered at `0x83D6`, named `ring_push_0990` in
[`ghidra-functions.csv`](ghidra-functions.csv). Re-run in `r2 -a 8051`
(§7's house method; `ec/tools/disasm8051.py` is the fallback, not the
authority):

```
0x83d6  9009bf   mov  dptr,#0x09bf
0x83d9  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x83da  c3       clr  c
0x83db  942f     subb a,#0x2f
0x83dd  4002     jc   0x83e1
0x83df  e4       clr  a
0x83e0  f0       movx @dptr,a          ; the counter is zeroed at 0x2f
0x83e1  9009bf   mov  dptr,#0x09bf
0x83e4  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x83e5  2490     add  a,#0x90          ; DPL = counter + 0x90
0x83e7  f582     mov  dpl,a
0x83e9  e4       clr  a
0x83ea  3409     addc a,#0x09          ; DPH = 0x09 (+ carry)
0x83ec  f583     mov  dph,a
0x83ee  ef       mov  a,r7
0x83ef  f0       movx @dptr,a
0x83f0  9009bf   mov  dptr,#0x09bf
0x83f3  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x83f4  04       inc  a
0x83f5  f0       movx @dptr,a
0x83f6  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x83f7  c3       clr  c
0x83f8  942f     subb a,#0x2f
0x83fa  4002     jc   0x83fe
0x83fc  e4       clr  a
0x83fd  f0       movx @dptr,a          ; and again on the way out
0x83fe  22       ret
```

The counter at `0x09BF` is held below `0x2F` at both ends, so it is `0x00`-
`0x2E` at `0x83E5`; `DPL` is then `0x90`-`0xBE` with **no carry**, and `DPH`
is exactly `0x09`. **`DPTR` is therefore bounded to `0x0990`-`0x09BE`**, which
is the routine's own name, and it **cannot reach `0x09E9`, `0x09EA` or
`0x09EB`.** That is a *positive* result — an exclusion derived from a decoded
bound rather than from an empty scan — and it is what licenses the
single-writer phrasing in §2 and in the two `registers.yaml` rows.

**And it is still one idiom.** The register form (`mov 0x83,...`, or
`mov DPH,a` from a register) and an indirect `movx @Ri` are equally invisible
to this grep; `mov DPH,a` (`F5 83`) appears 87 times in the EC image by
itself, and this hunt only asks what *follows* it in the immediate form. So
the honest form of the claim is: **among the sites this method finds that aim
a computed `DPH` at the `0x09` page, the one that exists cannot reach these
three addresses** — not "no other writer exists". A third writer reached by a
`DPTR` this grep cannot follow remains possible, and nothing here is a live
test that would have caught it.

`0x83D6` is itself a follow-up: it has no annotation row naming its caller
beyond the `0xAD9F` `ljmp` in
`reset_xdata_flags_and_07d5_to_ff` and the `0xCC78` run, and whether the ring
is ever pushed while the `0x0743` gate is open is not established.

## 4. The four-hop chain, end to end

This is the section the issue asks for: the path drawn as one thing, each hop
cited to a committed address rather than to another section. The two middle
hops are in two routines, and each is a short contiguous run — which is why
they can be written as code rather than as prose.

```
0x0745  CTGP_DB_TPP_TARGET  Windows service, per power mode
   |     windows/vendor-ec-map.md:84 -- SetGpuDynamicBoostTotalProcessingPowerTarget
   v
0x96ad  apply_oem_overrides_then_fill_08xx
0x96ed  900743   mov  dptr,#0x0743     ; CTGP_DB_CTRL
0x96f0  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x96f1  30e020   jnb  acc.0,0x9714     ; bit 0 clear -> skip both copies
0x96f4  900745   mov  dptr,#0x0745
0x96f7  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x96f8  9009ea   mov  dptr,#0x09ea
0x96fb  f0       movx @dptr,a          ; <== HOP 2: 0x09EA = [0x0745]
0x96fc  900746   mov  dptr,#0x0746
0x96ff  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x9700  9009eb   mov  dptr,#0x09eb
0x9703  f0       movx @dptr,a          ; <== and 0x09EB = [0x0746]
   v
0x83ff  sync_0788_and_07d4_from_09e9
0x8473  9009ea   mov  dptr,#0x09ea
0x8476  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x8477  9007d4   mov  dptr,#0x07d4
0x847a  f0       movx @dptr,a          ; <== HOP 3: CPUA = [0x09EA]
0x847b  9009eb   mov  dptr,#0x09eb
0x847e  e0       movx a,@dptr
0x847f  9007d5   mov  dptr,#0x07d5
0x8482  f0       movx @dptr,a          ; <== and DBAP = [0x09EB]
   v
0x07d4  CPUA / 0x07d5  DBAP
0x07c4  bit 4, set from CTGP_DB_CTRL bit 1 by 0x9711 lcall 0x94c0
0x07c4  bit 3 (DBEN), set to follow bit 4 at 0x8483-0x8496
   v
ASL:  T1WR Arg0 == 0x73 (evidence/acpi/dsdt.dsl:50700-50717)
      and its _Q84 twin (:52796-52803), both guarded by If ((DBEN == One)):
        Local0 = (^^PCI0.LPCB.EC0.CPUA * 0x08)   ->  ^^NPCF.ATPP
        Local1 = (^^PCI0.LPCB.EC0.DBAP * 0x08)   ->  ^^NPCF.AMAT
   v
_SB_.NPCF.ATPP / .AMAT  the NVIDIA platform controller
      declared External at dsdt.dsl:55,57; the device node itself is created by
      AML (^^^^NPCF.NPCF, ToUUID "36b49710-2483-11e7-9598-0800200c9a66",
      :52772, :52780) and carries no _ADR, so there is no bus address to
      cite for it and none is claimed here
```

Two things about this drawing that are easy to get wrong, and are the reason
it is worth having in one place:

- **The `0x07C4` bits gate hop 3, they are not part of the data path.** The
  `ATPP`/`AMAT` publish is guarded by `If ((DBEN == One))` in the ASL, and
  `DBEN` is bit 3 of `0x07C4`, which `0x83FF` itself makes follow bit 4 — and
  bit 4 is set from bit 1 of `0x0743` by the `lcall 0x94c0` at `0x9711`, the
  same routine as hop 2. So the guard travels *down the same `0x0743` byte* as
  the data. `0x0743` bit 0 gates hops 2 and 3 in the EC; bit 1 reaches the
  ASL's `DBEN` check through bit 4. `ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md` §3 and §4.1 are
  the full argument; this is the sketch.
- **Hop 3 is conditional in the EC as well as in the ASL.** `0x83FF` compares
  before it writes (`xrl a,r7 ; jnz 0x8473` at `0x8460`/`0x8465` and `0x846C`/
  `0x8471`), so the copy runs only when `0x07D4`/`0x07D5` differ from
  `0x09EA`/`0x09EB`, and only when bit 3 and bit 4 of `0x07C4` already agree
  does it fall through to the compare at all (§5). The ASL multiply by 8 and
  the EC's copy are both conditional; only the ASL's `DBEN` guard is named.

**What this establishes.** The chain is complete on static evidence: four
hops, two middle ones in one routine each, every byte cited to a `bank0`
address in a committed `.asm`, and the two ends cited to a committed
`windows/vendor-ec-map.md` line and to two decoded ASL sites in
`evidence/acpi/dsdt.dsl`. **What it does not establish** is that any hop ran
during the one committed capture that opened the gate — which is §5, and
which is a different question from whether the path is complete.

## 5. What the 2026-09-23 capture does and does not establish

`evidence/ec-watch/2026-09-23-power-mode-cycle-0700-07ff.csv` is the one
committed capture in which the gate this chain runs behind was opened. In
timestamp order, every row in that file touching the chain:

```
2026-09-23T17:57:41.307+02:00,0x0743,0x00,0x02   bit 1 set
2026-09-23T17:57:41.307+02:00,0x0745,0x00,0xFF
2026-09-23T17:57:41.307+02:00,0x0746,0x00,0x05
2026-09-23T17:57:51.161+02:00,0x07C4,0x08,0x28
2026-09-23T17:57:51.589+02:00,0x0743,0x02,0x03   bit 0 set -- the gate opens
2026-09-23T17:57:51.597+02:00,0x07C4,0x28,0x38   bit 4 set, 8 ms later
```

`0x07D4` and `0x07D5` have **no rows at all** in that file, and neither does
`0x09E9`, `0x09EA` or `0x09EB`. The natural reading is that the tail of the
chain did not run — but there is a third possibility the two-hypothesis
framing skips, and settling it needs the control flow rather than the CSV.

**`0x83FF` compares bit 3 against bit 4 of `0x07C4` before it compares
anything else.** Worked against the file's own `0x07C4` values, in the
`0x83FF` listing of `ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md` §3:

| `0x07C4` | `0x8441` (`jnb acc.4`) | `0x844E` (`jnb acc.3`) | `A` at `0x8458` | `0x8459` `jnz 0x8473` | compares reached? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `0x28` (b3 set, b4 clear) | taken -> `R7 = 0` | not taken -> `R6 = 1` | `1 ^ 0 = 1` | **taken** | **no** |
| `0x38` (b3 and b4 set) | not taken -> `R7 = 1` | not taken -> `R6 = 1` | `1 ^ 1 = 0` | not taken | yes |

So for the `0x28` state the copy at `0x8473` runs **unconditionally** — the
"already equal" explanation is not merely unproven there, it is *not
reachable*, because `0x8460`/`0x846C` are not on the path. That is a
statement about the instruction stream and needs no hardware.

**And the copy leaves a fingerprint, which the file does not carry.** Having
taken the branch, `0x83FF` falls into `0x8483`, where `0x8487` `jnb acc.4`
takes to `0x8490`-`0x8496`: `anl a,#0xf7` clears bit 3, so **`0x07C4` becomes
`0x20`**. The capture's next row for the byte is `0x28`->`0x38` at
`17:57:51.597`, and **no `0x07C4` row in the file carries `0x20`**. (`0x20`
does appear elsewhere in the file — six times, all for `0x070A` — so this is
a statement about the rows of that one byte, not about the value.)

That absence is suggestive and is **not** proof, for a reason the file's own
shape gives: `ec_watch.py` is a *sweeping* sampler, and the CSV is `ts,addr,
old,new` logged on a change between two sweeps. A value that existed entirely
between two sweeps is not written down. A `0x20` held for less than the sweep
interval, or one that came and went between the `0x28` and `0x38` samples,
would look exactly like this file does. This is the same endpoint-net blind
spot `docs/hardware-tests/gpu-tgp-07c4-07d7-door.md` §6 warns about, and the
correct reading of the missing `0x20` row is "the file carries no positive
trace of the copy", not "the copy did not happen".

**The `0x38` window is where the question is actually open, and this file
cannot speak to it.** From `17:57:51.597` to the end of the capture `0x07C4`
is `0x38`, which is the only state in which the compares run and the only one
in which `0x8471` `jz 0x849c` can return without writing. Two reasons it is
untestable here, and they have to be stated together:

- `0x09EA`/`0x09EB` are on page `0x09`, and this capture watched `0x0700`-
  `0x07FF` — the filename says so and every address in the file is in that
  range. The "already equal" hypothesis is a claim about a comparison between
  two values, and this file has an observation on **neither side** of it.
- The CSV records `old,new` on a transition, so it does not carry the values
  `0x07D4` and `0x07D5` held at any moment either. Even a `0x07D4` row would
  have been a before/after pair, not the pair of values the compare reads.

**A third route accounts for the silence without either hypothesis.**
`0x83FF` returns before touching `0x07D4`, `0x07D5` or `0x07C4` at all if
either early-out fires: `0x83FF`/`0x8402` reads `0x080F` and `0x8405`
`ljmp 0x849c`s if it is non-zero, and `0x8408` calls `0xB9D8` with `0x840D`
`ljmp 0x849c` if that returns non-zero. Neither `0x080F` nor `0xB9D8` has a
`registers.yaml` row, and neither is in the capture's window. `0x080F` is not
hypothetical — it is `xdata-registers.csv`'s `main-ec-264`, with 11 references
across 5 functions, and `bank0:0x9CA6` writes `0x00` to it at `0x9CB4`-`0x9CB8`
and decrements it at `0x9D01`-`0x9D03`, both in the same routine.

**So, stated at the strength the evidence carries.** The capture does not
establish that the tail of the chain ran, and it does not establish that it
did not. What it does establish is narrower:

1. The four-hop chain is complete on static evidence (§4), with the middle two
   hops in one routine each and every byte cited to a `bank0` address.
2. `0x07D4` and `0x07D5` did not move in the one committed capture that
   opened both gates, with the `0x09xx` half never watched. That is a weak
   negative, it changes neither entry's `status:`, and **a byte that did not
   move is not evidence the EC does not act on it** — it is evidence that
   nothing observed wrote it, and a passive capture is further from a live
   test than a live run is.
3. For the `0x28` state, "already equal" is *not reachable* — the compare is
   skipped, and the copy would have been followed by a `0x20` that the file
   does not carry. This is the one thing the capture settles, and it settles
   it by bounding a hypothesis rather than by confirming an effect.
4. For the `0x38` state the file is silent, and the step that would speak is
   a run watching `0x09E9`-`0x09EB` next to `0x07C4`-`0x07D7`.
   [`gpu-tgp-07c4-07d7-door.md`](../../docs/hardware-tests/gpu-tgp-07c4-07d7-door.md)
   is the prepared procedure and **as written it cannot do this**: its
   `WATCH` set covers `0x07C4`-`0x07D7` and `0x0743`-`0x0746` and no
   `0x09xx` address, so a run of it would still leave the "already equal"
   hypothesis without an observation on either side. That gap is recorded
   there as a follow-up rather than fixed here — extending the watch set
   would break the deliberate 24-address contract
   `windows/tools/test_gpu_block_watch.py` pins, and that is #94's to own.

**Nothing above is a live test, and no sentence here should be read as one.**
No register was written, read back, or observed changing. The committed CSV is
quoted as a committed file and nothing more. Running the door procedure is a
step for a human at the physical machine (CLAUDE.md, "Cloud agents cannot
reach the hardware"), and its status stays **not run**.

## 6. What is open

- **`0x09E9`'s second writer.** It has **two** direct write sites — `0x9747` in
  `0x96AD` and `0x9C0D` in `0x9C00` — where `0x09EA`/`0x09EB` have one each,
  and they are on different terms: `0x96AD` derives it from `0x0744` or from
  table bytes chosen by `0x0490`/`0x049F`, `0x9C00` clears it when `0x0743`
  bit 2 is clear. That is a different question from "what is it", and it is
  why **`0x09E9` gets no `registers.yaml` row in this change**. It is also the
  byte `0x83FF` syncs into `0x0788` (site `0x8425`, the compare this routine
  is named for), so it is not only a `0x0743`-shaped question.
- **`0x83D6`'s callers, and whether the ring is pushed with the gate open.**
  §3 bounds its DPTR and nothing more; what pushes it, and when, is not
  established.
- **`0x080F` and `0xB9D8`.** Both are early-outs in front of hop 3 (§5) and
  neither has a `registers.yaml` row. `0x080F` has 11 references across 5
  functions in the census; what its non-zero state suppresses is not decoded.
- **The `0x09xx` watch window.** Named in §5 and in the door procedure. Until
  a run covers `0x09E9`-`0x09EB` next to `0x07C4`-`0x07D7`, the tail of the
  chain is complete on paper and silent in the one capture that exists.
- **The `0x07D4`/`0x07D5` `GFID`/guard questions** are not reopened here;
  `ec-07c4-07d5-sites.md` §9 holds them, along with `0x07C5`/`0x07C6` (issues
  #106, #101) and `0x07D6`/`0x07D7` (`ec-07d6-07d7-sites.md`).
- **These two rows are `present-untested` and stay there.** Nothing in this
  file exercised the bytes, and the `status:` vocabulary is deliberate. The
  value of this walk is the chain and the bound, not a status change.

## 7. Spot-checks against an independent disassembler

`ec/tools/disasm8051.py` is a linear decoder, not a disassembler, so every
listing this file's argument rests on was re-run in radare2 (installed by
