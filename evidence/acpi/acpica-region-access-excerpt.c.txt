========================================================================
acpica-region-access-excerpt.c.txt -- what ACPICA does with the operand
of a `Field` read out of a `SystemMemory` operation region
========================================================================

Issue #1337 asked what the *interpreter* does with the unaligned operand
`MMRD` passes to `MMRW`, because `docs/findings/mmrd-unaligned-escape.md`
holds the ASL and the vendor driver and neither of those interprets AML.
This is the source the behaviour comes from: the ACPI reference
implementation, which the specification is written against and which the
Linux kernel builds a copy of. `linux-acpi-region-access-excerpt.c.txt`
is that copy; the two hold the same decision points in two independent
implementations, because the claim is that both behave the same way and
one of them doing it would not show that.

Each fragment below is a `sed -n A,Bp` range of the real upstream file, with
upstream's own line number as a prefix, so a line cited from the write-up
means the same line here and in the ACPICA tree. The fragments are here
because a claim rests on them, and they are bounded rather than whole
files for that reason -- `linux/patches/gm7mg7p-dmi-entry/upstream-excerpt.txt`
says why, and `fetch-acpi-sources.sh` re-derives every one of them from a
fresh fetch and diffs the result against this file.

What the write-up reads out of them, so a reader knows what to look at:

  * `AcpiExSystemMemorySpaceHandler` validates the access *width* and, on
    Itanium only, the access *address*. On anything else the pointer it
    loads from is derived from the requested address and nothing tests
    its residue against the width.
  * `MMRW`'s field is declared `ByteAcc`, and `AcpiExDecodeFieldAccess`
    turns that into an access width of 8 bits -- so the width reaching
    the handler is the access granularity, not the 32 bits the field
    declares.
  * `AcpiExExtractFromField` therefore issues one region access per datum,
    advancing the offset by the access width each time, which is four
    separate byte-wide accesses for a 32-bit field.
  * The route a field read takes to get there, so that "the field read is
    split" is checkable as a route and not only as a claim: the resolve and
    read-data fragments carry the first two edges, and the
    `AcpiExAccessRegion` banner carries the third.

Nothing here was run. These are static reads of public source, and they say
what the interpreter does with the operand -- not what this machine's
Windows `ACPI.sys` does, which is closed source and is recorded as a null
in `docs/findings/acpi-interpreter-region-access.md`.
