# `containment` at 1.00 cannot tell a copy from a fragment, so the size now
# runs the other way too

`ec/annotations/xdata-export-ownership.md` §6 offered the `containment` column
as the per-row evidence for a fold: "a reader is expected to look at it."
That column is `|A ∩ B| / |A|` with `A` the smaller body, so a four-statement
stub scores `1.00` against any larger body that happens to spell those four
statements. The score of a copy and the score of a fragment are the same
number, and `MIN_BODY_STMTS` does not separate them because it is a floor and
the cases are four to six statements.

This is the correction of that, in three parts and in the order they are safe
to apply:

- **`member_share` and `owner_body_lines` are committed columns.** They are
  derived from what `--map` already had in hand, so the map's shape does not
  move and `xdata_register_map.py`'s `OWNERSHIP` oracle still reads the same
  10,178 references.
- **`--min-share` is the ratio as a switch, and it ships off.** Refusing a
  containment edge when the member accounts for less than a named share of its
  owner changes which rows `scan()` opens, so turning it on re-pins every
  downstream figure. It is measured below and deliberately left to its own
  change.
- **Every row of the population has a verdict**, in
  `ec/annotations/xdata-export-ownership-verdicts.csv`, checked in both
  directions by `ec/tools/export_ownership_verdicts.py`.

## What the evidence says, and two corrections to the issue

Re-derived by joining each `shared=yes` row's `body_lines` against its owner's,
from the committed map:

| predicate | rows |
|---|---:|
| `containment` = 1.00 **and** owner ≥ 2 × member | **25** |
| `containment` ≥ 0.90 **and** owner ≥ 2 × member | **30** |
| all non-owner rows | 146 |

**The count is 25, not 22** — 30 if the containment bound is read as `≥ 0.90`
rather than `= 1.00`. The issue's rows are all present in the population; three
more satisfy the issue's own predicate. The tool's predicate is the committed
one and this page carries the issue's 22 beside the derived 25 rather than
editing the issue away.

**The three stubs are joined to `common/0806.c` by a *direct* edge, not by a
chain.** Their containment against the owner is 1.00, so `classes_of()` unions
them on the strength of that one edge. The mechanism is therefore sharper
than the issue described it: not a chain that a chained member's own score
would have exposed, but a single directed edge that scores 1.00 by
construction, which is exactly why the score could not have distinguished it.

### The three ways a `fragment` was established

Each is a different kind of evidence, and none of them is "the containment
score was low":

- **The owner's own text calls the member.** `common/30FB.c` calls
  `FUN_CODE_322c()` and `increment_xdata_0a4d_then_ljmp_311d()` four times
  each, and `common/3402.c` calls `FUN_CODE_3451()` once. A routine the owner
  calls is not an export of it.
- **The owner's committed bytes do not cover its own decompilation.**
  `bank0/BB6A.asm` runs 0xBB6A–0xBB77 and ends on a store with no `ret` and no
  store to 0x0855/0x0856; its `.c` continues into `bank0/BB78.asm`'s eight
  bytes at 0xBB78. `bank0/F078.asm` is one instruction, `inc DPTR`, and its
  `.c` carries the bodies of the routines at 0xF08B, 0xF0F3 and 0xF499 — the
  three rows above it in the table. Both are the function-boundary defect
  reaching the containment comparison, not a property of the EC.
- **The member's bytes are a routine with callers of its own.**
  `common/3BDD.asm`, `common/4368.asm` and `common/4AFB.asm` are all
  `c2 8e c2 8f d2 ab 22`, reached by four, two and five callers, disjoint
  across the three — 15 `lcall` sites across 11 callers, as their plate
  comments already record. `common/0806.c` ends with the same four statements
  spelled inline, which is a *call* to that logic that the decompiler wrote
  out, and it is why the containment edge is unavoidable from the text alone.

## Calibration: what this does and does not cost

- **The three stubs cost the census nothing.** `occurrence_re()`'s vocabulary
  is `('DAT_EXTMEM', 'symbol')` and `common/3BDD.c`, `4368.c` and `4AFB.c`
  match **zero** tokens each; across the whole population the total is 54
  tokens. Folding them loses no reference. The harm is that the map and the
  page assert an ownership claim the fold does not support — a false claim,
  not a lost byte. "Zero tokens" is a measurement of this method's vocabulary,
  not a claim about what the EC does with a byte.
- **`body_lines` counts `;`-delimited fragments, not statements.**
  `bank0/8026.c` reads 6 for four statements and two local declarations, and a
  nested `if (...) {` splits into a fragment of its own. `member_share`
  inherits that unit, so it is a ratio of fragment counts and inherits
  everything that says about them. Re-normalising it moves every figure on
  `xdata-export-ownership.md` and both oracles, and is its own change.
- **No mechanical criterion decides the population**, and that is measured,
  not assumed. Asking whether the member's bytes lie inside the owner's range
  says yes for one non-owner row of the whole map — and not one of the
  42-file class, which is known to be a single routine — because the
  committed `size` column is itself a product of the boundary defect:
  `bank1/8001.c` is 3 bytes for the 393-byte run it decompiles, and
  `bank0/F078.c` is 1 byte for a 14-statement body. Asking whether the
  member's decompiled text is a literal slice of the owner's says no for
  `common/0877.c`, which plainly is the tail of `common/0902.c` with one
  `_d_5 = 0;` interleaved. So the verdict is a person reading two committed
  files, and the ledger's job is to make sure that reading exists for every
  row and has not drifted from the map it was recorded against.

## There is no window a floor can land in, and why it ships off

`--min-share` refuses an edge when the member accounts for less than the named
share of its owner. Measured on this tree:

| floor | non-owner rows | largest class | owner | files the census would read again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| off (committed) | 146 | 42 | `bank1/8001.c` | — |
| 0.25 | 134 | 42 | `bank1/8001.c` | 12 |
| 0.30 | 133 | 42 | `bank1/8001.c` | 13 |
| 0.50 | 118 | 42 | `bank1/8001.c` | 28 |

At 0.30 the 42-file class is untouched, and the 13 files handed back to
`xdata_register_map.py --export-ownership` account for 78 address tokens — of
which the three stubs are three files and no tokens.

**There is no separating window, and that is the reason the column ships
committed and the floor ships measured-and-off.** `member_share` does not
order the two shapes. The `fragment` verdicts in the ledger run from
`common/3239.c`'s `0.06` — 3 fragments against `common/30FB.c`'s 53 — up to
`bank0/BB78.c`'s `0.50`, 3 against `bank0/BB6A.c`'s 6; `0.18` is the three
stubs' 4-of-22 and the smallest share among *those three*, not among
fragments. And the two shapes meet at the top instead of sitting either side
of a line: `bank0/BB78.c` is a `fragment` at `0.50` and `common/3BBF.c` is a
`re-export` at the same `0.50`. Refusing every `fragment` edge needs a floor
above `0.50`, and at that floor the `re-exports` go with them. No value on
this column separates the two shapes; it measures size, and the fold needs
something no size ratio answers.

`0.34` — `bank1/80EF.c`, 16 fragments against `bank1/8001.c`'s 47 — is the
lowest share among the 42-file class's *members*, and it does not bound a
window either. The floor conditions the edge while the class is a connected
component, so a member under the floor is still held in the class by a chain
of edges that each clear it — the same non-composition caveat as the eight
`bank0/F0*.c` members above, and why the class survives floors well past
`0.34`. That is why `SHARE_ORACLE` in `export_ownership.py` pins the cost at
a *named* candidate value instead: there is no threshold for the map to hand
over, so enabling the floor is a decision somebody makes with the numbers in
front of them, and it re-pins `xdata_register_map.py`'s `OWNERSHIP` oracle
deliberately.

## What is deliberately not here

- **No default flipped.** `--min-share` ships off and `--export-ownership`
  stays off. Enabling the floor re-pins the 10,178-reference oracle and is its
  own change, with `xdata-export-ownership.md` §5's argument written down
  first.
- **The three stubs are not folded together.** That is `--fold-equal`'s job
  and the sibling issue's. The ledger records a negative verdict against
  `common/0806.c` and needs nothing from either.
- **No function-boundary fix.** `--mode rebuild-project` and a database write
  that cannot share a branch; `xdata-06c2-06db-timers.md` §8 item 7, still
  open. This is the measurement it is argued from.
- **Nothing needed hardware or Windows, and no live observation is claimed.**
  Every figure here is a property of committed text, re-derivable by re-running
  the tools.
