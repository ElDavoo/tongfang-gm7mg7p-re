# Does the vendor service write any `0x08xx` byte, and how far did we look (issue #334)

`docs/hardware-tests/level-block-0860-086e.md` §5 names this question and
declines it: *"Whether the service writes any `0x086x` byte at all is
answerable statically and is not asked here."* It was answerable; this is the
answer, and what matters about it is the reach attached to it.

**The answer: not found.** No EC call site in the decrypted service resolves
into `0x0800`-`0x08FF`, no caller of any of the helpers that take the address as
a parameter resolves there, and no `ECSpec` constant in any of the three
committed managed trees names an address in that band. **The word is "not
found", not "does not":** the four boundaries that separate those two are the
second half of this write-up, and the method's reach differs per tree by more
than the answer does.

```console
$ python3 windows/tools/ec_addr_reach.py
$ python3 windows/tools/ec_addr_reach.py --addr 0x086B
$ python3 windows/tools/ec_addr_reach.py --self-check
```

No live test ran and none is claimed: there is no laptop and no Windows machine
reachable from a GitHub-hosted runner. Everything below is a static property of
committed text, re-derivable with the commands beside it.

## The reach, and where the `0x08xx` hole is

The committed census (`windows/decompiled/v3.1.39.0/ec-callsites.csv`,
regenerated by `ec_callsites.py`, which `windows/vendor-ec-map.md` documents) is
the input this question turns on. Its 868 rows carry an address on 866 of them,
and those addresses run `0x0408` to `0x0FE0` in exactly three high-byte bands:

| high byte | sites | what lives there |
|---|---|---|
| `0x04xx` | 13 | temperature and BIOS-info bytes |
| `0x07xx` | 700 | everything the service actively drives: mode, PLs, fan table, GPU bytes |
| `0x0Fxx` | 153 | the fan-table walks |

**`0x08xx` is not one of them, and neither are `0x05xx` or `0x06xx`.** That is
the shape of the answer: the service's EC writes cluster in the page the power
mode lives in, and the level block's page is one of the seven high bytes the
census skips entirely between `0x07xx` and `0x0Fxx`.

A hex-literal grep is not how you would find that, and the issue is right about
this: `ECSpec` constants are C# `const`, so the compiler inlined them and the
call sites carry **decimal**. `0x086B` is `2155`.

```console
$ grep -rE '0x0?86[0-9A-Fa-f]' --include=*.cs windows/decompiled/ | wc -l
0
```

That zero is exactly the "a static scan finding zero references means *not
found by this method*" case `ec/annotations/registers.yaml` warns about, and
on its own it would be worth nothing. It is the *shape of the histogram*, read
by a tool that resolves decimal literals, that carries the answer.

## The parameterised path, resolved

The census's `addr_kind` column is 723 `literal`, 143 `computed` and 2
`unresolved`. The two `unresolved` rows are the helper bodies of
`SingleZone.ReadECRAM` and `SingleZone.WriteECRAM` — private methods taking
`ushort Addr` and forwarding it straight to `MyEcCtrl.Instance.Read` /
`EcCtrl.Write`. **A `0x08xx` write through that helper is the one shape the
committed CSV structurally cannot hold**, since the address is not in the row
at all. It is where an unresolvable `0x08xx` write would hide, so the tool
enumerates every caller and resolves its first argument.

The helper set is *derived* from those two rows rather than listed by name, and
that is not tidiness. The tree also holds
`MyRgbLightBarDefault.WriteECRAM(int, uint, uint, uint, uint, uint)` in another
class: six lightbar levels, no address, and a name-based sweep would collect its
four call sites as parameterised EC addresses. Deriving from the census's own
unresolved rows gets both the membership and the scoping right, because a
helper that forwards a parameter to `EcCtrl.Write` *is* a row the census could
not resolve.

Every caller resolves, to eight distinct addresses:

| decimal | byte | `ECSpec` name |
|---|---|---|
| 1895 | `0x0767` | `ADDR_TRIGGER_BYTE` |
| 1897-1899 | `0x0769`-`0x076B` | `ADDR_RGBKB_LEVEL_R/G/B` |
| 1900-1902 | `0x076C`-`0x076E` | `ADDR_RGBKB_LEVEL_DEFAULT_R/G/B` |
| 1989 | `0x07C5` | `ADDR_AP_OEM_BYTE5` |

All eight are in `0x07xx`, and **every one of them is a named `ECSpec`
constant**, so the two lines of evidence close on each other rather than
standing as two unrelated zeros. The helper is the RGB-keyboard path;
`SingleZone` extends `HIDKeyboard`, and this board's chassis uses HID rather
than the lightbar the sibling classes drive.

### A second parameterised door, and it is a dead one

`WMIEC.WMIReadECRAM` and `WMIEC.WMIWriteECRAM` (`MyControlCenter/WMIEC.cs` and
`MyRGBKeyboard/WMIEC.cs`, two classes, four methods) also take an EC address as
a parameter — but they reach the EC over WMI, through a `ManagementObject`
`GetSetULong` invocation, not through `EcCtrl`. **No call site of theirs is a
row in the census at all, and neither is their definition**, which is why they
are listed in the tool rather than derived from it.

Swept tree-wide: **zero callers, anywhere in the committed tree.** That is a
dead door rather than a live blind spot, and the distinction is the useful
part — a helper nobody calls cannot hide an address. Had it had callers, this
would have been the one genuinely uncovered route into the band.

## The `ECSpec` table, as an independent line

`ECSpec` is C# `const`, so a constant naming an `0x08xx` byte would already be
inlined into its call site and would already be in the census. Reading the table
separately is therefore not a second search — it is a check on the search, and
one that needs no call-site parser to be right.

In all three trees (`v3.1.6.0`, `v3.1.39.0`, `v3.9.18.0`) the `const ushort`
values span `0x0001` to `0x07FB` and fall in `0x00xx`, `0x04xx` and `0x07xx`.
**Zero in `0x0800`-`0x08FF`** — across all three committed trees the vendor
never *names* a constant in that band at all, which is a statement about the
constant tables and not about what the code does with an address it builds some
other way.

The `const ushort` requirement is doing real work, and its limit is worth
naming too. It excludes the same file's `const uint` fan-mode codes and its
`[Flags]` enums, whose members are byte masks — `User_Fan_Level1 = 0x81` is an
enum member whose value is a plausible address, and a bare number scan would
report it as one. What it does **not** exclude is the `0x00xx` band, which is
entirely WMI event codes (`OSD_FanModeSwitch = 0xB0` and its siblings) rather
than addresses. That makes the claim above the stronger one: it is over every
named constant the file carries, of which the addresses are a subset.

## The boundaries — what this zero does not cover

Every zero in this write-up is **"not found by this method"**, in the sense
`CLAUDE.md` and the caveat on `ec/annotations/registers.yaml` both insist on.
None of them is "absent", and the method's reach differs per tree by more than
the answer does. Four boundaries, and the first is the one a reader is most
likely to skip.

1. **The two partial trees are not this answer.**
   `ec_callsites.py` over `windows/decompiled/v3.1.6.0` and
   `windows/decompiled/v3.9.18.0` yields **zero** call sites each — not zero in
   `0x08xx`, zero. Those trees are anti-tamper casualties
   (`windows/antitamper/README.md`): most `GCUService.exe` method bodies do not
   decompile, and a body that did not decompile is a body that was not
   searched, not one that does nothing. `t1wr_callers.py`'s marker census is
   the measurement — ILSpy's own `Invalid MethodBodyBlock` errors, counted in
   the `.cs` text, of which the decrypted service carries none and the two
   partial trees carry 27 and 326. **Pooling the three zeros would produce a
   claim none of them supports.** The answer above rests on `v3.1.39.0` alone;
   the other two are reported separately and are the reason this list exists.

2. **A binary immediate.** This is a text scan of decompiled C#. A numeric
   immediate in native code is invisible to it — the blind spot
   `t1wr_callers.py`'s own header names for a string scan, in the case where
   MSVC emits `'ECRW'` as a four-byte immediate. Nothing here searches
   `windows/decompiled/native/` or `vendor/`.

3. **The EC firmware itself.** Whether the EC reads or writes a byte in the
   band is a question about `ec/firmware/GMxMGxx_11.800`, asked of
   `ec/tools/scan_refs.py`, not of this tool. A `0x086x` byte that only the
   firmware touches is exactly the live hypothesis the level-block capture
   exists to test, and this write-up says nothing about it. Nothing in
   `ec/annotations/registers.yaml` changes as a result: a static negative about
   the Windows service is not evidence about what the EC does with a byte, so
   `XDATA_086x` and `XDATA_0860` stay `present-untested`.

4. **The unbounded computed index.** The 143 `computed` sites are
   `(ushort)(3840 + i)`-shaped table walks with bases at `0x0F00` through
   `0x0FE0`. `ec_callsites.py` cannot bound `i` and this tool does not either,
   so each is reported as its **base** and marked unproven. A walk from a
   `0x0Fxx` base is not an `0x08xx` site; it is also not a proof that none
   exists.

   Two things bound the population without bounding the index, and both are
   weaker than the index bound would be. Every computed site is in a fan-table
   manager — `FanTable_Manager1p5`, its `_CML` sibling, `FanTable_Manager`, or
   `FanTable_Manager2` — and no other class contributes one. And the bases above
   `0x0F5F`, which is where `FanTable_Manager1p5` puts its CPU and GPU rows per
   `windows/vendor-ec-map.md`, belong entirely to `FanTable_Manager2`: a class
   `MyFanTableCtrl` does not instantiate on this board. Neither fact bounds `i`
   inside a walk, and the write-up does not lean on either of them.

## What it changes for the level-block capture

`level-block-0860-086e.md` §5 makes the vendor service the confound: the service
rewrites the whole bundle on every mode switch, so a `0x086x` move observed with
the service up is "not yet the EC's", and §3's service-stopped second pass is
the arm that separates them. §5 said the static question was open.

It is now closed, in the direction that makes §3's second pass **corroboration
rather than the load-bearing half**. Within the reach above, a `0x086x` move in
the main arm is not the service writing that band: its resolved writes are in
`0x04xx`, `0x07xx` and `0x0Fxx`, and the bundle it rewrites on a switch is
entirely `0x07xx`/`0x0Fxx`.

**That is a demotion, not a cancellation.** The negative is scoped, and the
scoping is what the four boundaries are for. A live capture attributes by
observation; a static zero attributes by absence of a hit within a stated
reach. §3's second pass is still a human's session at a machine this
repository cannot reach, and it still separates the service's *whole-bundle*
rewrite from the level block's own dispatch by showing it — which is a
different question from whether the service has a writer in the band, and the
one the capture actually answers.

## Reproducing the negative

`ec_addr_reach.py --self-check` asserts the claims this write-up makes against
the committed tree, and each refusal is exercised by
`windows/tools/test_ec_addr_reach.py` against a broken copy of the tool's own
measurement. The positive control is the point of that suite: a scratch tree is
planted with an `0x086x` write in each of the three spellings a real one could
take — a decimal through the parameterised helper, a decimal straight through
`EcCtrl`, and a hex literal — and the sweep is held to find all three. **A
negative whose search cannot be shown to find a planted address is worth
nothing**, and nothing in the tool itself can say it would have.
